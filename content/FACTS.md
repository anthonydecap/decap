### MIDI conversion units (Decap product)
- Double-valve MIDI unit, 16 double-switching valves per unit, placed between key frame and main
  chest: book/roll operation stays completely intact; book and MIDI can play at the same time.
- Minimal dimensions, mounts in any orientation (even upside down).
- Any of the 16 MIDI channels; each valve assignable to any MIDI note with the included software
  (keeps tubing short and simple).
- Note length adjustable from −64 to +64 ms; note delay up to 500 ms (e.g. to sync with piano
  systems that need 500 ms pre-delay).
- Customers buy the units from Decap; installation by Atelier Frank Decap (or their own builder).

### Organ pipes (Decap product): a headline message
- **Decap is the world's only company that developed CNC manufacturing of organ pipes in-house**
  (user's claim; use it). **Almost any wooden pipe can be made and shipped within a few days to a week, depending on the ranks.**
- Sizing: our own standard scales, or the customer's own measurements.
- For everyone: DIY projects, your own organ (new ranks, replacements, extensions), organ builders
  and restorers. Customers can buy only pipes, or pipes plus wind chests and more from Decap.
  "A lot of possibilities."
- Wooden pipes: bourdons, flutes, violins, reed pipes. Made on 5-axis CNC with Decap's own
  **PipeCreator** software (generates the G-code); in-house since 2016.
- Body in Ayous (Triplochiton scleroxylon): stable, pale yellow, virtually knot-free. Foot and mouth
  in hand-selected beech.
- Custom measurements with short delivery times; voicing by Decap's voicers or left to the customer.
- Range (MIDI note numbers):
  - Stopped: Bourdon 24–96 (6 octaves); Jazz Flute 60–96 (3 oct.); Vibraton 46–84 (3 oct.);
    Double Street Organ 60–96 (3 oct.)
  - Open: Flute (Flute 8′ etc.) 60–96 (3 oct.); Violins (alto, cello etc.) 48–96 (4 oct.)

## Orchestrionix: Brass Core (existing Prismic page `core`, to become `brass-core`)

- A voice-coil motor converts an audio signal into physical vibrations inside a pressure chamber,
  replicating a player's embouchure; the real brass instrument's resonance shapes the tone.
- Works with standard mouthpieces and tubing: trumpet, trombone and similar.
- Non-invasive: no drilling or modification of the instrument.
- Input: line-level audio (page lists 3.5 mm stereo line-in and mini XLR; external amplifier).
  12 V DC, 2 A; 120 × 90 × 70 mm. Accessories: trumpet clamp, trombone clamp.
- Users: theatres, sound designers, museums, studios, collectors. Testimonial on page: Circus Ronaldo.
- **Brass Core is one of the few off-the-shelf products: it ships immediately when in stock.**
  Show its price: **€ 2,599** (confirmed; the shop's Stripe product still says € 799 and must be corrected).

## smARTvalve (existing Prismic page `smartvalve`, keep)

- One valve per pipe with its own processor; airflow regulated more than 1,000 times per second;
  pressure 0–200% stored per pipe; 4 ms response; PID feedback; optical MIDI cable (100× MIDI speed,
  200 m+); LFO effects, vibrato, slope, decay. Proven in L'Explorateur (2025).
- Optical MIDI is an accessory of smARTvalve.
- **smARTvalve is fully digital:** every valve has its own processor, and every pipe is individually
  addressable. With Decap's software, **stops are defined in software ("digital stops")**: any
  combination of pipes can form a stop, in practically unlimited combinations, each pipe with its
  own pressure settings. (user, 2026-10-20)
- **smARTvalve retrofit:** valves are fitted to a pipe panel, so an existing organ can be retrofitted
  by replacing its pipe panels. In a full smARTvalve organ everything is software (stops and
  mappings unlimited); when smARTvalve extends an existing organ, the limits of the original
  controller/control system apply, case by case. smARTvalve is extremely small (exact dimensions to be supplied by
  Decap). (user, 2026-10-20)
- **From Yves Rechsteiner's own article** ("About the Explorateur", studio31.bearblog.dev): idea in
  2013 (artistic director of Toulouse les Orgues); met Tony Decap in 2019 through a teaser of
  Stephan Eicher's Die Automaten; construction from 2021; first valve generation (IPU) after two
  years; smARTvalve developed end 2023 to end 2025, designed by Tony Decap. The valve regulates the
  wind "a few millimetres from the pipe's foot": it measures the pressure drop on the input side and
  opens further to compensate, so pressure stays stable when many pipes sound. No regulators
  needed: a blower and a distribution box. Set-up with smARTvalve "less than 2 hours from the truck
  arriving to playing". Built to fit a 20 m³ van, max. 1,200 kg. More than 30 performances since
  2023 (churches, museums, theatres; repertoire incl. Vierne, Fauré, Poulenc's Organ Concerto).
  Residencies with the duo Gamut (Berlin) and George Rahi (Toronto). Rechsteiner: organ builders
  consider smARTvalve a high-performing, responsive solution.
- **Any MIDI can be mapped to smARTvalve:** e.g. the key position can drive the valve opening, so
  smARTvalve can behave exactly like a position-following ("linear") valve, as one mapping among
  countless others (velocity, aftertouch, CC, pressure curves, LFOs…). (user, 2026-10-20)
- **For organ builders (B2B):** no public prices. Message: the technology is available and goes
  further than any other valve. Never name competitors (e.g. Orgelpunkt Linear Valve L1) on the site;
  compare categories (on/off valve, position-following valve, closed-loop smARTvalve) instead.

## Who we are and what sets Decap apart (from Decap, 2026-10-26) — overrides anything above

**Real dance organs.** Decap Herentals is known for organs, and specifically for **real dance
organs**: organs built for people to dance to. Not so much street organs or barrel organs; Decap has
built those too, but normally it builds dance organs.

**The golden age (1960–1980).** In that period there were some **120 dance cafés and dance halls**
("baancafés" / dancings) where people went to dance every day, and each had an organ: a dance
organ. This is **across Belgium** (confirmed by Decap).

**Musicians first, not carpenters.** At heart Decap are **musicians first**. They learned
woodworking in order to build organs, but they are musicians before anything else. That set them
apart from the rest then, and it still does. Since Decap Herentals began, the aim has been to make
the organ play better and better: **more feeling, more control over volume and intensity, more
dynamics, more musicality.** ("De constante jacht naar de beste muzikale ervaring": the constant
hunt for the best musical experience.)

**Dynamic control, all developed in Herentals** (unique Decap developments):
- **Percussion**: a Decap drum is dynamically controlled. The stick can hit hard or soft, and
  timings can be adjusted. On a traditional organ the stick is driven by air pressure and always
  hits with the same intensity.
- **Wind chests**: traditional wind chests use ordinary standard valves, so a pipe is simply on or
  off. On a Decap organ the **pressure can be adjusted per wind chest**, for different intensities,
  and pipe ranks can play effects such as **vibrato**.
- **smARTvalve**, the newest development, takes this concept to **every individual pipe**.
- **Volume control**: Decap Herentals organs and orchestrions are **volume-controllable**.

**Why it matters.** Because of all this, Decap organs don't only play in dance halls: they are used
**in the living room as well as on big stages, from theatre shows to DJ sets**. With traditional
mechanical technology this would not be possible. **Decap Herentals keeps evolving.**

**MIDI.** Decap Herentals were **the first to bring MIDI to the organ** (milestone 2000 in the
timeline; Tony to confirm the year — note that Mostmans' Antwerp history mentions a MIDI organ in
1996). Today almost all old organs are converted to MIDI, simply because it is the better way to
play: it is how you get a much wider repertoire on your organ.
- At first there was resistance: installing MIDI takes away part of the organ's romance, the
  **book** (the folding cardboard music that runs through the organ).
- **Decap's answer, recently:** the **Decap Virtual Orchestrion** player can **project the book
  fully digitally**, so the book comes back on screen while the organ plays from MIDI.

**From dance organs to smARTvalve (from Decap, 2026-10-26).**
- All the experience built up over **90 years** (use "90 years"; Decap Herentals dates from 1933)
  and the constant hunt for the best musical experience led to the **smARTvalve** development.
- **For the last 25 years** (since around 2000) Decap has built **more than dance organs**:
  orchestrions and self-playing instruments, from **separate pipe chest units** to **automatic
  drums** and **automated pianos** (in practice: Decap drives existing systems such as QRS; **keep this vague**
  on the site, e.g. "automated pianos", without claiming Decap builds them), and also **technology for church organs**.
- **Why church organs.** "We are musicians, and the church organ is one of the most beautiful
  instruments there is, and one of the most musical." While developing smARTvalve, Decap could only
  dream of what it would do for a church organ: far more effects and ways of playing, **far more
  colours, far more musical possibilities**.
- **Dance organ experience pays off here too.**
  - Dance organs had to be **transportable and rebuildable**: Decap builds every organ as **modular
    units in a façade**. That is why a project like **L'Explorateur** (a transportable church
    organ) fits Decap perfectly.
  - Decap wind chests are **built for hard conditions**, such as changes in temperature, because
    dance organs played in **spiegeltents (mirror tents)** that had to be put up and taken down
    again and again.

**All software in-house (from Decap, 2026-10-26).** All Decap software is developed entirely
in-house: from its own **CNC programs** (PipeCreator, which generates the G-code for the pipes),
to the **hardware control** (the software that drives the instruments, e.g. in the Accordion
Pedestal, the MIDI conversion units and smARTvalve), to the **player software**
(Virtual Orchestrion, MIDIMapper).
